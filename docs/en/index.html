Built from an October 2026 indicator study: 48 indicators, observed on 2014–2019 and tested on 2020–2026. Single overheating readings (RSI, distance from moving averages, greed) were on average followed by further gains, and combined overheating had mixed outcomes — neither is a reliable sell signal; the bull-market dip watch shows no reliable difference from ordinary bull-market days (after 2020 its triggers did worse; the earlier edge came mostly from two 2017 cases) — a state reference only. The weekly Supertrend (ATR 10 × 3, from Bitstamp daily candles, completed weeks only) flips about once a year with few whipsaws, but it confirms rather than predicts — by the time it turns down, price is usually weeks past the high. Holder behavior

Cost basis and profit/loss of long- and short-term holders. There is no free, republishable full history (free APIs cover about 4 years at most), so they aren’t scored.

STH cost basis · STH-MVRV

Not verifiableFree to view

The average purchase price of short-term holders (coins held under 155 days). Above it, recent buyers are in profit and bull-market pullbacks often stall nearby; below it, they are underwater. STH-MVRV = price ÷ this cost basis, and STH-NUPL = 1 − 1 ÷ STH-MVRV (below 0 means below cost).

Caveat“Below cost in a bull market = a good entry” only works if you already know it is a bull market; in bear markets price stays below this line for months.

CheckOnChainBGeometrics

STH cost basis vs True Market Mean (cross)

Tested · not adoptedFree to view

The True Market Mean (TMMP) is the average cost of coins still in active circulation. When the short-term holder cost basis crosses above it, this is often read as “bull market confirmed”: recent buyers are paying above the market average; a cross below is read as a confirmed bear market.

CaveatWe tested it in October 2026 with CheckOnChain’s history: the last three crosses above came 190–490 days after the cycle bottom, with price already 135–239% above it — always later than the weekly Supertrend turning up; the ratio correlates 0.85 with MVRV Z-score; it whipsawed in May 2016 and April 2020; there are only 5 events. A confirmation signal, not an entry signal.

RULES

Rules

  1. AlertWhile holding, top signal ≥ 50 or top timing ≥ 50 → on alert (stays until a sale).
  2. SellOn alert, the weekly Supertrend (ATR 10 × 3) turns down → sell everything.
  3. Buy backIn cash, bottom signal ≥ 50 or the weekly Supertrend turns up → buy everything back.

Comparison: early-top version (added 2026-10-15; does not replace the main strategy): one extra alert condition — at least 650 days since the cycle low and a new all-time high within the last 180 days (no heat, no clocks). It covers the blind spot of a top arriving a year or more before the clocks expect. In walk-forward backtests its trades are identical to the main strategy; with the clocks shifted a year late to simulate early tops, the main strategy rides the 2014 and 2022 bears while this version sells after each top (plus one extra mistaken sale in Nov 2019).

The top signal and timing light up early, so they only put the strategy on alert; the actual sale waits for the weekly trend to confirm. Top signal = heat × timing ÷ 100 never exceeds timing, so the alert condition is effectively “top timing ≥ 50” and heat itself does not affect trades. Thresholds are fixed at 50, not fitted to history; all 27 combinations of 40/50/60 beat holding, but they produce only 3 distinct position paths — not 27 independent tests.

LIMITATIONS

Limitations

  • It does not sell the top: it waits for trend confirmation, and past sales came 26–65% below the peak. The edge comes from sitting out bear markets and buying back near bottoms.
  • It makes mistakes (a bad sale in 2021-05). There is no stop-loss, so drawdowns of −70% to −80% can still happen while holding (from a 2018 start, the max drawdown equals holding’s).
  • Small sample: only four bear markets to test; if the four-year cycle breaks, the strategy may fail.
  • The edge is concentrated early: 7.5× holding from 2014, but only 2.1× from 2018, 2.1× from 2020 and 1.5× from 2022.
  • An early top in 2028 is a blind spot: the alert effectively depends only on top timing, which the clocks put no earlier than 2029-02-01. If the top comes in 2028, the strategy won’t go on alert and will ride the whole decline. Historically, “heat ≥ 75, timing < 30, 150–450 days after the halving, ≥ 650 days after the low” occurred in only 4 stretches: Nov 2013 was the cycle top, but after Jan 2021, Feb–Mar 2021 and Nov–Dec 2024 price rose a further ~99%, ~80% and ~38% (Nov 2024 was ~200 days after the halving, right after the election, with heat at 87) — so heat alone can’t flag an early top (tested Oct 2026).
  • So an “early-top version” (see rules) is tracked separately from 2026-10-15 in its own append-only record (data/strategy_ledger_early.csv). Historically it never differed from the main strategy; differences can only appear after 650 days from this cycle’s low (Apr 2028) while the clocks have not arrived — and a sharp mid-bull drop in that window could shake it out.
  • Walk-forward: each cycle uses only the tops and bottoms known at the time; the latest cycle uses the site's current signals. But top dates are assigned in hindsight (no one could confirm a top on the day), and data revisions aren’t versioned, so “no look-ahead at all” is not yet proven.
